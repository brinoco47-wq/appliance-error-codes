What to Do Before Troubleshooting an Appliance

Guide · 2026-10-18

Most appliance injuries happen before the repair starts — on a machine that is still powered, still connected to water, or still hot. Run this routine first, every time.

1. Cut the power

Unplug the appliance. If the plug is not reachable, switch the circuit off at the breaker and confirm the machine is dead (no lights, no display). Never work on a machine that is only switched off at its own controls — internal parts can still be live.

2. Cut the water

For washers and dishwashers, turn the supply taps off before disconnecting any hose, and keep towels and a tray ready. A litre or more of water hides in hoses and filters.

3. Mind the gas

For gas dryers, ovens, and water heaters, know where the gas shut-off is before you start. If you smell gas at any point: do not touch electrical switches, leave the building, and call your gas utility or emergency services from outside.

4. Let it cool

Ovens, dryers, and water heaters stay dangerously hot long after they stop. Give them time, and test with the back of your hand near — never on — the surface.

5. Know your stop line

Decide in advance what you will not do: no opening sealed panels, no bypassing safety switches or door locks, no touching gas valves or high-voltage parts without qualifications. See when to call a qualified technician.
