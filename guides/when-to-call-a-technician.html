When to Call a Qualified Technician

Guide · 2026-10-17

DIY checks fix a large share of appliance faults — blocked filters, kinked hoses, unbalanced loads. Some signs mean the safe move is to stop and call a professional.

Stop and call if you see any of these

  • A burning smell, scorch marks, or smoke. Unplug (if safe to reach the plug) and do not run the machine again.
  • The same code returns immediately after every basic check. The cause is a failed part or sensor, not a blockage.
  • Water anywhere near electrics — a leak onto a motor, control board, or outlet.
  • Gas work of any kind — valves, connectors, burners, or a suspected leak.
  • Sealed refrigeration systems — compressors, refrigerant lines, and sealed-system repairs need licensed handling.
  • High-voltage parts — microwave capacitors hold a dangerous charge even unplugged; oven and dryer heating circuits are not beginner work.
  • The machine is under warranty. Opening it can void the warranty — call the manufacturer first.

What to tell the technician

Have ready: the exact error code, the model number, what you already checked, and when the fault appears (every cycle, only on spin, only when hot). That short list often saves a second visit.
